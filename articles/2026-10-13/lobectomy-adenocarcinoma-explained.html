Lobectomy and Lung Adenocarcinoma: A General Explainer

Teaser: Lobectomy and lung adenocarcinoma, explained from NCI, the American Cancer Society and Cleveland Clinic. No stage has been announced. Holy See bulletin: https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2026/10/13/261013b.html

This is general information, not medical advice. It describes lung cancer and lung surgery in general. It is not a statement about the Holy Father's condition, stage, treatment or outlook. Only his doctors and the Holy See Press Office can speak to those.

FACT: What the Vatican has said (and what it has not)

The Holy See Press Office reported that "the lower lobe of the right lung was removed using a video-assisted technique," and that "The intraoperative histological examination confirmed the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma, as suspected by the pre-operative tests." (Holy See Press Office)

No stage has been announced. Vatican News reports that "In the coming days, post-operation exams will be conducted on the removed tissue." (Vatican News) Nothing below should be read as a prediction for him.

CONTEXT: General medical background

What is lung adenocarcinoma?

The National Cancer Institute (NCI) describes two main types of lung cancer, non-small cell and small cell. It says non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) "is more common than small cell lung cancer." (NCI) Adenocarcinoma is one kind of NSCLC: NCI calls it "a type of lung cancer that begins in the cells that line the alveoli and make substances such as mucus" (NCI).

The American Cancer Society (ACS) says that "About 80% to 85% of lung cancers are NSCLC" and that "Adenocarcinoma is the most common type of non-small cell lung cancer." (ACS)

What is a lobectomy?

NCI explains that "Each lung has sections called lobes," and that "The right lung is slightly larger and has three lobes." (NCI) It defines lobectomy as "surgery to remove a whole lobe (section) of the lung." (NCI)

According to the ACS, when a lobectomy can be done, "this is often the preferred type of operation for NSCLC." The ACS adds that "Doctors often prefer to do a more extensive operation (for example, a lobectomy instead of a segmentectomy) if a person's lungs are healthy enough, as it may provide a better chance to cure the cancer." (ACS)

What does "video-assisted" mean?

The ACS describes video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) as a minimally invasive approach. It "uses smaller incisions, typically has a shorter hospital stay and fewer complications than a thoracotomy" (open-chest surgery) (ACS). Cleveland Clinic notes that for a VATS or RATS procedure, surgeons "make several small cuts in your chest for access without spreading your ribs." (Cleveland Clinic)

What does "intraoperative histological examination" mean?

NCI's Dictionary of Cancer Terms defines histology as "The study of tissues and cells under a microscope." (NCI Dictionary) NCI also explains that in diagnosing lung cancer, "A pathologist then views the sample under a microscope to look for cancer cells." (NCI)

Staging, in general

NCI says: "The process used to find out if cancer has spread within the chest or to other parts of the body is called staging," and "It is important to know the stage in order to plan treatment." (NCI) NCI also lists the factors that prognosis and treatment options depend on. They include the stage, the type of lung cancer, certain gene mutations, symptoms, and "your general health" (NCI). Again, the Vatican has announced no stage.

General recovery ranges (not a forecast for any individual)

  • Hospital stay: The ACS says, "Generally, you will need to spend 1 to 7 days in the hospital depending on the type of surgery." (ACS) Cleveland Clinic says, "With a VATS or RATS lobectomy, your hospital stay is typically two to three days." (Cleveland Clinic) These are general ranges for patients in general. Separately, for this specific case, Vatican News reports that Bruni "confirmed that Pope Leo is expected to remain in the hospital for about a week after surgery." (Vatican News) We draw no conclusion from the difference; only his doctors speak to his case.
  • Overall recovery: The ACS says, "Recovering from lung cancer surgery typically takes weeks to months," and that "People who have VATS instead of thoracotomy tend to have less pain after surgery and to recover more quickly." (ACS) Cleveland Clinic says, "It takes at least a month to recover from a lobectomy." (Cleveland Clinic)
  • Follow-up: Cleveland Clinic describes "a follow-up visit seven to 21 days after your surgery." (Cleveland Clinic)

Possible further treatment, in general

NCI explains that after surgery, "some people may be given chemotherapy or radiation therapy after surgery to kill any cancer cells that are left," which is called "adjuvant therapy." (NCI) Whether any such treatment applies to anyone depends on the factors NCI lists above. Nothing about further treatment for the Holy Father has been announced.
