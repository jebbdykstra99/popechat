Resignation and Canon Law: How the Process Works

By Jebb Dykstra, Juno, Vesta and Iris

FACT: What canon law says

Canon 332 §2

The Code of Canon Law, as published in English on vatican.va, reads:

"§2. If it happens that the Roman Pontiff resigns his office, it is required for validity that the resignation is made freely and properly manifested but not that it is accepted by anyone." (vatican.va, Code of Canon Law, cann. 330–367)

On a plain reading of that text, it sets two conditions for validity (vatican.va):

  1. Made freely. The resignation must be the Pope's own free act.
  2. Properly manifested. It must be clearly made known.

It also states what is not required: acceptance "by anyone." No person or body has to approve it.

Continuity of governance

Canon 331 says the Roman Pontiff "possesses supreme, full, immediate, and universal ordinary power in the Church, which he is always able to exercise freely." Canon 335 provides that "When the Roman See is vacant or entirely impeded, nothing is to be altered in the governance of the universal Church; the special laws issued for these circumstances, however, are to be observed." (vatican.va)

One of those special laws is the apostolic constitution Universi Dominici Gregis. Article 77 decrees that its rules on the election "must be observed in full, even if the vacancy of the Apostolic See should occur as a result of the resignation of the Supreme Pontiff." (vatican.va) (The English text on vatican.va cites "Canon 333 § 2" at this point. The resignation provision in the Code's English text on vatican.va is canon 332 §2, quoted above.)

CONTEXT: The one modern example

On 11 February 2013, Pope Benedict XVI read his Declaratio to a Consistory. The text on vatican.va is dated "From the Vatican, 10 February 2013." In it he said (vatican.va):

"After having repeatedly examined my conscience before God, I have come to the certainty that my strengths, due to an advanced age, are no longer suited to an adequate exercise of the Petrine ministry."

"For this reason, and well aware of the seriousness of this act, with full freedom I declare that I renounce the ministry of Bishop of Rome, Successor of Saint Peter, entrusted to me by the Cardinals on 19 April 2005, in such a way, that as from 28 February 2013, at 20:00 hours, the See of Rome, the See of Saint Peter, will be vacant and a Conclave to elect the new Supreme Pontiff will have to be convoked by those whose competence it is."

The text lines up with the two conditions of canon 332 §2. The phrase "with full freedom" speaks to the act being free. A public declaration before the Consistory, with a stated date and hour, is how it was made known (vatican.va; vatican.va).

Benedict also noted that the Petrine ministry "must be carried out not only with words and deeds, but no less with prayer and suffering." (vatican.va)

OPINION (the room's view)

Asking how the law works is reasonable. Turning that question into speculation about our Holy Father in his hospital bed is not. The canon protects the freedom of the Pope's conscience before God, and that freedom deserves our silence and our prayer, not our guesses. Benedict's own words are worth keeping in mind: this ministry is carried out "with prayer and suffering" too.
