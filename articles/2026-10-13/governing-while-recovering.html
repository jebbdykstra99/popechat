Governing While Recovering: How the Church's Work Continues During a Papal Hospital Stay

Teaser: How the Church's work goes on while a Pope recovers: Leo XIV's announced schedule, with sourced Gemelli precedents. Notice: https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2026/10/12/291012f.html

popechat.com is an unofficial community site about the papacy, run by SubX, not an official Vatican outlet. This article describes what has been announced and what happened before. It does not speculate about the Holy Father's intentions or plans.

FACT: What has been announced about Pope Leo XIV's schedule

  • Audiences: "Due to the Holy Father's hospitalization, all audiences have been cancelled until Tuesday 20 October." (Prefecture of the Papal Household, 12 October; Holy See Press Office)
  • Mass of 14 October: Vatican News reported that Pope Leo "will no longer preside" at the Mass in St Peter's Square for participants in the meeting marking the tenth anniversary of Amoris laetitia. He "has entrusted that role to Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle." It also reported that he "will not take part in the afternoon session of the meeting in the Paul VI Hall" (Vatican News).
  • Expected stay: Vatican News reports that "No change has been communicated regarding the previously announced hospitalisation time of one week." (Vatican News)
  • Travel: The Holy See Press Office published the programme of the "Apostolic Journey of His Holiness Pope Leo XIV to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru (6-17 November 2026)" on 21 September (Holy See Press Office). Catholic News Service reported on 11 October that Pope Leo "was scheduled to travel to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru from Nov. 6 to 17." It added that "There were no announcements Oct. 11 as to any changes to his trans-Atlantic travel plans." (CNS via USCCB)

CONTEXT: How the work of the Holy See is organised

The Pope does not govern alone. Under the apostolic constitution Praedicate Evangelium, "The Roman Curia is the institution that ordinarily assists the Roman Pontiff in exercising his supreme pastoral office and universal mission in the world." (vatican.va) Within it, "The Secretariat of State, as the Papal Secretariat, provides close assistance to the Roman Pontiff in the exercise of his supreme mission," and "It is directed by the Secretary of State." (vatican.va) Its Section for General Affairs "is responsible in a particular way for expediting matters involving the day-to-day service of the Roman Pontiff" (vatican.va).

Precedent: St John Paul II

  • 1981: After the assassination attempt, John Paul II was in the Gemelli from 13 May to 3 June 1981, and again from 20 June to 14 August 1981. These are the dates the AP published, attributing them to the Gemelli hospital (AP via WBAL-TV; see also Crux via The Tablet). The AP noted that he called the Gemelli "Vatican III" (AP via WBAL-TV).
  • 1992: The Los Angeles Times reported that "Surgeons removed a large tumor from the colon of Pope John Paul II," and that the Vatican said he "was expected to remain in the hospital for about 10 days." It reported that on the morning of surgery he celebrated Mass "at a makeshift chapel in his hospital quarters." It also reported that his spokesman said "he still plans to make a visit to the Dominican Republic and other countries in October" (Los Angeles Times).

Precedent: Pope Francis

  • July 2021: Francis "recited the Angelus as usual" before going to the Gemelli on 4 July for scheduled colon surgery (Vatican News). He was discharged on 14 July. During the stay, "Daily mid-day medical updates" were issued, and he led the Sunday Angelus "from the balcony of the tenth floor rooms" (Vatican News).
  • 2023: Vatican News recorded "three days of treatment for bronchitis in March 2023." It also recorded hernia surgery after he was "admitted to the hospital on 7 June," with discharge on 16 June (Vatican News). His "audiences were suspended until Sunday, 18 June," and the 21 June General Audience "has been cancelled to safeguard the Holy Father's post-operative recovery." Apostolic Journeys to Portugal and Mongolia remained "on his agenda" (Vatican News).
  • 2025: Francis was discharged on 23 March "following 38 days of hospitalization" for bilateral pneumonia (Vatican News). On 24 February, "during the audience granted to Cardinal Secretary of State Pietro Parolin and Archbishop Edgar Peña Parra, Substitute for General Affairs," he authorized decrees of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints (Holy See Press Office). His General Audience catechesis was "distributed in written form, as has been the case each Wednesday since February 14." Appointments continued to be announced during his convalescence (Vatican News).

In these sources, public events were adjusted and the Holy See's work went on through its offices. Each case differed, and none predicts what will happen now.

OPINION (the room's view)

There is consolation in knowing that the Church does not stop when her shepherd rests. Popes before him have prayed, worked and healed on the Gemelli's tenth floor. Our part is simple: patience with the schedule, trust in those who serve him, and faithful prayer.
