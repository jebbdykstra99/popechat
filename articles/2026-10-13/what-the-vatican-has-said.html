What the Vatican Has Said: Pope Leo XIV's Lung Surgery, 11–14 October 2026

Teaser: Pope Leo XIV, Gemelli: Vatican News https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-10/pope-leo-surgery-gemelli-hospital-successful.html Holy See bulletin: "The operation was successful" https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2026/10/13/261013b.html

popechat.com is an independent Catholic community site. It is not an official Vatican outlet. This page quotes only the Holy See Press Office and Vatican News, and every quotation was checked against the live page on 14 October 2026. All dates are Rome dates.

FACT: The official timeline

Sunday 11 October: the Pope's own announcement

After the Angelus, Pope Leo XIV told the faithful in St Peter's Square: "Dear friends, during some recent medical examinations, the doctors found a small, localized nodule in my lung. It was detected at a very early stage and is well-localized. The doctors believe that it is best to intervene, so, following their advice, I have decided to undergo surgery to remove it. The operation is already scheduled to take place in the coming days. I thank you for your spiritual closeness and entrust myself to your prayers. Thank you, and happy Sunday!" (Vatican News)

Answering journalists, Press Office Director Matteo Bruni said "the operation is scheduled for the first days of this week and the estimated post-operative recovery time will be about one week." (Vatican News)

Monday 12 October: audiences cancelled

The Prefecture of the Papal Household announced: "Due to the Holy Father's hospitalization, all audiences have been cancelled until Tuesday 20 October." (Holy See Press Office)

Vatican News reported that Bruni "clarified that the examinations had been routine checks," and that the Pope "held all his scheduled audiences on Monday morning." (Vatican News)

Tuesday 13 October: admission

The Press Office told journalists: "As announced, Pope Leo XIV has been admitted to the Agostino Gemelli University Hospital. This afternoon, he will undergo surgery for a nodule in the lung, performed by the thoracic surgery team led by Professor Stefano Margaritora." (Holy See Press Office)

Vatican News described the surgery as one to remove a "small, localized nodule" from his lung. It reported that the Pope "arrived at Gemelli Hospital at 1:07 p.m." and that Bruni "confirmed that Pope Leo is expected to remain in the hospital for about a week after surgery." (Vatican News)

Tuesday 13 October, evening: the medical bulletin

The Press Office published a statement from Dr Luigi Carbone and Professor Stefano Margaritora (Holy See Press Office):

"The Holy Father underwent thoracic surgery as scheduled."

"To ensure a radical procedure, the lower lobe of the right lung was removed using a video-assisted technique. The intraoperative histological examination confirmed the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma, as suspected by the pre-operative tests."

"The Holy Father was in the operating theatre for approximately three hours; the operation itself lasted about ninety minutes. The operation was successful and there were no complications."

"Following the operation, the Pope woke up normally and, after a brief period of observation, returned to his room on the tenth floor."

The bulletin itself refers to "The Holy Father" and does not name the hospital; the Gemelli and the Pope's name come from the Press Office note of 13 October and Vatican News, cited above.

The bulletin adds that "The surgical team was led by Professor Stefano Margaritora." (Holy See Press Office)

Vatican News added that "Following the operation, Pope Leo asked for dinner and spent the evening in the company of his secretaries and other Vatican staff, according to the Director of the Holy See Press Office, Matteo Bruni." (Vatican News)

The same report notes: "In the coming days, post-operation exams will be conducted on the removed tissue." It also says "No change has been communicated regarding the previously announced hospitalisation time of one week." That report said the Press Office would give an update on Wednesday morning, and it did (see below). (Vatican News)

Wednesday 14 October, morning: the night

Vatican News reported that the Holy See Press Office said on Wednesday: "The night passed calmly for the Pope. Further information will be provided later today." (Vatican News)

The same report notes: "No change has been communicated regarding the previously announced hospitalization time of one week." (Vatican News)

Wednesday 14 October, afternoon: the doctors' statement

Vatican News reported that the Press Office released a statement from Dr Luigi Carbone and Professor Stefano Margaritora on Pope Leo XIV's postoperative recovery (Vatican News):

"This morning, the Holy Father underwent the blood tests and chest X-ray scheduled after surgery. The results confirm that his postoperative recovery is progressing normally: the values are within the expected range for the type of operation performed."

"The Holy Father has undergone respiratory physiotherapy and has begun walking around his room. He has resumed eating normally and had breakfast and lunch."

"His recovery is proceeding as expected."

Vatican News also reported that Matteo Bruni later told journalists the Pope concelebrated Mass on Wednesday afternoon "in a room specially prepared for the purpose on the 10th floor of Gemelli Hospital." (Vatican News)

What has not been announced

No official source cited here has given a stage for the cancer. Vatican News said on 14 October that "In the coming days, post-operation exams will be conducted on the removed tissue." No results of those exams had been announced at the time of this update (Vatican News).

CONTEXT

The Press Office issued its own statements and a statement issued by the physicians, while the Director answered journalists' questions. For 14 October we could not find the Press Office's own English text of the doctors' statement on its website, so we quote it as Vatican News published it. Where the Press Office's English text and Vatican News' rendering of the bulletin differ slightly (for example, "radical procedure" against "radical resection"), we have quoted each source exactly as it appears (Holy See Press Office; Vatican News).
