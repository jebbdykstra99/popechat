Peter, Part 2: The Rock

By the popechat editors

popechat.com · Story 01b · Urbi et orbi.

popechat is an unofficial community room about the papacy, run by SubX. It is not an official Vatican outlet, it does not speak for the Church, and it does not replace Mass. Scripture is quoted from the New American Bible, Revised Edition (NABRE) as published by the USCCB at bible.usccb.org; "…" marks a cut. Every quotation and date below carries its source.

How to read this story. Passages marked IMAGINED RETELLING picture Simon Peter as if he lived today. They are not history. They paraphrase the Gospel scenes named in brackets after each one, and they put no new words in his mouth. Passages marked THE RECORD say what the sources actually say, and where readers disagree we say so.

Where did the name Peter come from?

IMAGINED RETELLING. His brother finds him and tells him he has found the one they have been waiting for, and brings him along to meet him. The man looks at Simon and gives him a new name on the spot, as if he already knows who Simon will be. Anyone who has ever been handed a nickname they had to grow into knows the feeling. [Andrew brings Simon; the new name: Jn 1:40-42.]

THE RECORD. John's Gospel says Andrew "first found his own brother Simon and told him, 'We have found the Messiah' (which is translated Anointed). Then he brought him to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, 'You are Simon the son of John; you will be called Cephas' (which is translated Peter)" (Jn 1:41-42, NABRE).

The NABRE note explains the name: "the Aramaic word kēpā' meaning rock and transliterated into Greek as Kēphas is the name by which Peter is called in the Pauline letters" (except, the note adds, in Gal 2:7–8), and it is translated as Petros ("Peter") in Jn 1:42 (NABRE note on Mt 16:18). Pope Benedict XVI pointed out how unusual this was: "It was not Jesus' practice to change his disciples' names," and apart from the nickname "sons of thunder" for the sons of Zebedee, "He never gave any of his disciples a new name. Yet, he gave one to Simon, calling him 'Cephas'." He added that the name was translated "precisely because it was not only a name; it was a 'mandate'" (Benedict XVI, General Audience, 7 June 2006).

What happened at Caesarea Philippi?

IMAGINED RETELLING. They are on a road trip up north, far from the lake. Their friend asks what people are saying about him, and the answers come back: a prophet, someone from the old stories. Then he asks them directly. Simon answers, and gets told that he did not work it out by himself. Then he gets his new name explained, and a set of keys. [Caesarea Philippi and the question: Mt 16:13-15. The answer, the name and the keys: Mt 16:16-19.]

THE RECORD. Near Caesarea Philippi, which the NABRE note places "about twenty miles north of the Sea of Galilee" (NABRE note on Mt 16:13), Jesus asks, "But who do you say that I am?" (Mt 16:15, NABRE). "Simon Peter said in reply, 'You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God'" (Mt 16:16, NABRE). Jesus answers: "Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah. For flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my heavenly Father" (Mt 16:17, NABRE). Then:

"And so I say to you, you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of the netherworld shall not prevail against it. I will give you the keys to the kingdom of heaven. Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven; and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven" (Mt 16:18-19, NABRE). "Then he strictly ordered his disciples to tell no one that he was the Messiah" (Mt 16:20, NABRE).

Mark's version of the scene is shorter. Peter answers, "You are the Messiah," and there is no saying about the rock or the keys (Mk 8:29-30, NABRE). The NABRE notes that "the promise of the keys is given to Peter alone," while binding and loosing are given to all the disciples in Mt 18:18, where the note says the context suggests "the power of excommunication alone is intended" (NABRE note on Mt 16:19).

What does "this rock" mean?

Christians have read the verse in more than one way.

  • The Catholic Church's teaching. The Catechism says: "The Lord made Simon alone, whom he named Peter, the 'rock' of his Church. He gave him the keys of his Church and instituted him shepherd of the whole flock." It adds that this "pastoral office of Peter and the other apostles belongs to the Church's very foundation and is continued by the bishops under the primacy of the Pope" (Catechism of the Catholic Church 881, Vatican.va).
  • Peter's faith, in the same Catechism. Elsewhere the Catechism also says: "On the rock of this faith confessed by St. Peter, Christ built his Church" (CCC 424, Vatican.va).
  • The NABRE's note. The note reads Peter and the rock together. It says the presumed Aramaic would be "You are the Rock (Kēpā') and upon this rock (kēpā') I will build my church," that the church "will have Peter as its solid foundation," and that this function "consists in his being witness to Jesus as the Messiah, the Son of the living God" (NABRE note on Mt 16:18).
  • St. Augustine. In his tractates on John, Augustine reads the rock as Christ and Peter's confession of him: "On this rock, therefore, He said, which you have confessed, I will build my Church. For the Rock (Petra) was Christ"; in the same section he says the Church "was personified in the Apostle Peter, on account of the primacy of his apostleship" (Augustine, Tractates on the Gospel of John, Tractate 124, sec. 5, New Advent).
  • A Lutheran reading. The Lutheran Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope (1537) holds that "the Church has not been built upon the authority of man, but upon the ministry of the confession which Peter made, in which he proclaims that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God" (Treatise, para. 25, Triglot Concordia translation, BookOfConcord.org).

popechat is a community room about the papacy, and the Catechism is the Catholic Church's teaching. The other readings come from their own sources and traditions. Each is quoted in its own words, side by side, and this story does not rule between them.

What happened when Peter walked on water?

IMAGINED RETELLING. It is the middle of the night, the boat is fighting a headwind, and someone is walking toward them across the water. Everyone else is terrified. Simon is the one who asks to get out of the boat, and he actually does it. A few steps in, he notices the wind, panics and starts to go under. He shouts for help, and a hand grabs him. [The night crossing and the headwind: Mt 14:22-25. The disciples' fear: Mt 14:26. Peter's request, his steps and his sinking: Mt 14:28-30. The rescue: Mt 14:31.]

THE RECORD. "During the fourth watch of the night, he came toward them, walking on the sea" (Mt 14:25, NABRE), between 3 and 6 a.m. by the NABRE note's reckoning (NABRE note on Mt 14:25). "Peter said to him in reply, 'Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.' He said, 'Come.' Peter got out of the boat and began to walk on the water toward Jesus. But when he saw how [strong] the wind was he became frightened; and, beginning to sink, he cried out, 'Lord, save me!' Immediately Jesus stretched out his hand and caught him, and said to him, 'O you of little faith, why did you doubt?'" (Mt 14:28-31, NABRE; the bracketed word is the NABRE's). Then "those who were in the boat did him homage, saying, 'Truly, you are the Son of God'" (Mt 14:33, NABRE).

Only Matthew has Peter on the water. The NABRE note says Matthew "has inserted into the Marcan story (Mk 6:45–52) material that belongs to his special traditions on Peter (Mt 14:28–31)" (NABRE note on Mt 14:22-33).

Was Peter impulsive?

Pope Benedict XVI thought so. "Simon appears in the Gospels with a determined and impulsive character: he is ready to assert his own opinions even with force (remember him using the sword in the Garden of Olives: cf. Jn 18: 10ff.). At the same time he is also ingenuous and fearful, yet he is honest, to the point of the most sincere repentance" (Benedict XVI, General Audience, 17 May 2006). Three scenes show it.

IMAGINED RETELLING: telling the boss he is wrong. Soon after getting the keys, Simon hears his friend say that he is going to Jerusalem to suffer and be killed. Simon pulls him aside to tell him that can never happen, and gets a sharp rebuke in front of the others. [Mt 16:21-23; Mk 8:31-33.]

THE RECORD. "Then Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him, 'God forbid, Lord! No such thing shall ever happen to you.' He turned and said to Peter, 'Get behind me, Satan! You are an obstacle to me. You are thinking not as God does, but as human beings do'" (Mt 16:22-23, NABRE). Benedict XVI put it this way: "Peter, impulsive as he was, did not hesitate to take Jesus aside and rebuke him" (General Audience, 17 May 2006).

IMAGINED RETELLING: talking to fill the silence. On a mountaintop, overwhelmed by something none of them can explain, Simon does what nervous people do: he starts making practical plans out loud, offering to put up three shelters. The Gospel adds that he hardly knew what to say. [Mk 9:2-6.]

THE RECORD. "Then Peter said to Jesus in reply, 'Rabbi, it is good that we are here! Let us make three tents: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.' He hardly knew what to say, they were so terrified" (Mk 9:5-6, NABRE).

IMAGINED RETELLING: swinging first. When armed men come for his friend at night, Simon is the one with a weapon, and he uses it. He is told to put it away. [Jn 18:3-11.]

THE RECORD. "Then Simon Peter, who had a sword, drew it, struck the high priest's slave, and cut off his right ear. The slave's name was Malchus. Jesus said to Peter, 'Put your sword into its scabbard'" (Jn 18:10-11, NABRE).

Why do people see themselves in the rock?

IMAGINED RETELLING. Because the man given the name Rock is also the one who sinks, who argues, who says the wrong thing at the worst moment, and who swings first. He keeps getting caught, corrected and kept. [Mt 14:30-31; Mt 16:18, 22-23; Mk 9:5-6; Jn 18:10-11.]

Benedict XVI called it "a long journey for Peter that made him a trustworthy witness, 'rock' of the Church" (General Audience, 24 May 2006). The hardest part of that journey comes next. Part 3, The Denial, follows him into the high priest's courtyard on the night of the arrest (Mk 14:54, 66, NABRE).
